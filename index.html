The gap

Arrivals are up. The money still stops at the hotel gate.

The Point newspaper put it plainly in November 2026: tourists come for peace, but they want it in service delivery too — “fair treatment without harassment or scams.” The value leaks because nobody can prove who is legitimate.

01

Guests can't verify anyone

A visitor lands with no way to tell a licensed guide from a beach hustler. So they don't book anything — and the trip stays inside the resort.

02

Good operators are invisible

Excellent small guesthouses and guides have no page, no prices and no reviews. They lose the booking to whoever shouted loudest at the airport.

03

No price = no trust

Hidden prices read as a scam. Operators who publish an honest rate card convert far better — but almost none of them do.

Questions

Straight answers.

What exactly does “verified” mean?

We confirm the business exists, that the person we're dealing with controls it, and that they hold whatever licence applies to their category (tourism guide licence, guesthouse registration, or a national ID for drivers). We publish what we checked and the date. We do not guarantee a stay — we verify identity and licence, and we remove anyone who misbehaves.

Do I have to pay commission on bookings?

No. Starter and Growth are flat monthly fees. If you later want Kelloo to send you guaranteed bookings, we offer an optional 5% on bookings we route — but it's opt-in, never automatic.

I don't have a website or good photos.

That's the normal case, and it's fine. We build the page from what you have — phone photos included — and edit up to ten images on the Growth plan.

Can I take payment without a card machine?

Yes. Most bookings in The Gambia settle in cash or by Wave. Your page can show a Wave number for deposits and a Stripe link if you want card payments from European guests.

Is this only for the winter season?

The season is when operators have cash, so that's when we sell. The page keeps working all year, and annual plans are discounted before the season ends.
